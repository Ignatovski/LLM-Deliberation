"Cyber Finding Exchange" is a text-only negotiation testbed where three expert reviewers jointly assess a closed evidence packet and converge on the most defensible finding label and severity.

The parties are: "Risk Reviewer R", "QA Reviewer C", and "Technical Analyst K".

All evidence is visible from the start. The evidence packet is minimal and line-addressable. Every claim that matters should be grounded in those line IDs, not in outside assumptions.

Talks are turn-based, concise, and transparent in public. Your scratchpad and plan remain private. Your public message must stay inside <ANSWER>...</ANSWER>. Your structured assessment must be hidden inside <ASSESSMENT>{...}</ASSESSMENT> and still included in the JSON answer field.

Public negotiation is cooperative in the sense that the committee wants one defensible assessment, but each role has a different review lens. Repetition is allowed if justified by the packet. Do not reveal private notes or plans in the public message.

The research goal is to detect where committee trust fails: wrong consensus, drift, severity miscalibration, and weak evidence use.
